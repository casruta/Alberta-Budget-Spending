# Continuing-care access: archived outcomes

`continuing_care_access.csv` separates admission timeliness from point-in-time waitlist counts. Every page reference uses physical PDF page numbering, starting at one, from form-feed-separated pdftotext output; filenames containing Budget are internally official annual reports. Full raw pages are preserved alongside the CSV.

Admission within 30 days: 64% 2011–12,67% 2012–13,69% 2013–14,60% 2014–15,60% 2015–16,56% 2016–17,52% 2017–18. The 2011–15 values are retrospective values in the 2015–16 report chart(PDF 105), corroborated where overlapping by 2016–17 PDF 128 and 2017–18 PDF 122. Current-year targets documented are 70% 2015–16,62% 2016–17,65% 2017–18. Earlier target fields remain blank. No latest outcomes are imputed.

**Denominator:** the 2016–17 report PDF 148 says: “The result is calculated based on the number of individuals admitted to a Continuing Care Living Option within 30 days of their Assessed and Approved date divided by the total number of individuals admitted to a Continuing Care Living Option ... during the reporting period.” Spaces in that quotation are compacted for readability; the source-page file preserves exact text. The 2017–18 PDF 147 repeats the definition. These percentages concern admitted clients, not all waiting clients. Those never admitted during the period are outside the denominator.

**Waitlist counts:** contemporaneous 2011–14 measures use assessed-and-approved individuals waiting at March 31 in acute/subacute beds or the community(2013–14 PDF 142). Counts and percentages must not be spliced into a common series. Waitlist targets are fewer persons, while timeliness targets are higher percentages.

**Policy breaks:**2013–14 PDF 114 reports Edmonton/Calgary changed their waitlist processes inQ 3/Q 42012–13; the community list stopped adding clients waiting for their first facility of choice, producing a drop.2015–16 PDF 105 and 2017–18 PDF 122 discuss theMay 2015 Designated Living Option access/waitlist policy and preference-based placement, compared with the First Available Bed policy rescindedJune 2013. The chart records a 9 pp drop between 2013–14 and 2014–15 while discussion invokesMay 2015 implementation; retain that official discussion without assigning an exact policy-onset causal effect to the preceding fiscal year.2016–17 PDF 128 cites delayed openings, curtailed home-care investment and water incidents as factors. These are source explanations, not an estimated causal decomposition.

**Coverage:**2014–15 source is financial-only in the local archive inspected; its timeliness actual comes from later official charts. The 2018–19 archive does not establish 2018–19 admission-timeliness actual here. This work covers fiscal 2011–12 through 2017–18, not a current UCP outcome series. Ageing/demand, clinical need, staffing, choice and flow matter; timeliness is not a direct facility count or spending efficiency estimate.

**Additional contextual capacity evidence:**2011–12 PDF 108 explicitly says spaces increased by 1,002;2013–14 PDF 114 says 335 net new spaces versus 887 the previous year, with Southern Alberta flooding closing capacity. These statements can contextualize historical access, but have different scope from contemporary capital-plan delivery counts and are not a complete modern stock series.
