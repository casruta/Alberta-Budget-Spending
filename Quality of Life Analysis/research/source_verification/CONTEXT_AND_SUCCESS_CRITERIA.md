# What this assessment can establish

The user's question is whether Alberta's budget improves Albertans' quality of life. This is a province-wide public-policy assessment, not an investment recommendation. The financial observation window is the latest locally auditable ten fiscal years, 2015–16 through 2024–25. Outcome windows follow the actually observed government series; they are not forward-filled into 2025 or 2026. Fiscal 2025–26 ended before this October 2026 review, but neither its actuals nor its publication status is established by the available source files.

The evidence loop begins with budget inputs, then asks whether residents experience better access, educational outcomes, economic purchasing power and safety. Government performance measures are evidence of outcomes within their stated populations, not a complete definition of quality of life. Direct poverty and recent period life expectancy were subsequently recovered from Statistics Canada, closing those initial gaps within source scope. Current life satisfaction, healthy life expectancy, housing cost burdens, food insecurity and distribution by income/Indigenous status/region remain distinct requirements. A provincial average cannot establish that all Albertans benefited.

## Success rules fixed before integration

1. **Observed direction:** compare like-defined observations within the same current report wherever possible. Changes are descriptive, not automatically significant or causal.
2. **Published target:** compare a result only with the target for the same measure and observation period. A missed target is a documented performance shortfall, not evidence that every service failed. No invented thresholds are used.
3. **Access and quality together:** a higher service count or larger facility is an output/capacity observation; it does not substitute for wait times, health status, learning or housing outcomes.
4. **Resource context:** distinguish consolidated functional expense, ministry operating expense, capital investment and debt. CPI-adjusted spending per resident is a sensitivity for broad input intensity, not a health-price index, education spending per student, or efficiency measure.
5. **Distribution:** retain rural/urban differences and acknowledge missing subgroup series. Do not collapse unlike rates into a synthetic quality-of-life score or a target-hit percentage.
6. **Attribution:** a causal budget-success claim would require program exposure, lag structure, comparable outcomes, changing need/case mix, other funding and an appropriate counterfactual. This observational report does not identify that effect.

## Source discovery and authority

The supplied official PDF corpus comprises full older Government of Alberta annual reports and short newer year-end financial summaries. Misleading local “Budget” filenames do not override document identity. Existing repository notebooks' demographic assumptions are not used as authoritative inputs.

In this loop, the official Alberta annual-report and budget-document web indexes were retrieved successfully over HTTPS. Linked ministry PDF downloads were attempted individually: Health, Education and its result update, Mental Health and Addiction, and Public Safety and Emergency Services supplied new evidence. Some other linked reports returned 403. A successful index does not establish the contents of a blocked linked report. The record distinguishes website discovery, actual downloads, local archived sources and unresolved sources.

Live sources are public government publications; no credentials or stakeholder messaging are necessary. Official PDFs are closest to reported performance definitions and targets. Statistics Canada data quoted in Alberta reports are attributed as published there; this is not a claim that Statistics Canada's live tables were independently downloaded. Record exact pages, quotes, source hashes, dates and revision caveats. Preserve source inconsistencies instead of silently fixing them.

## Selection and limitations

Domains were chosen for their relevance to residents' daily lives, not because their results favor one government. Within retrieved reports, show both improved and deteriorated access measures and preserve missing outcomes. This is a focused assessment, not an exhaustive audit of every ministry performance measure. A mixed selection cannot be averaged into an overall score. A pre-2019 historical measure is background unless a comparable current series is recovered; it cannot describe current conditions by extrapolation.

The Data report-app preparer/runtime is unavailable in this execution context, as recorded in the completed Infrastructure Analysis workflow. The retained deliverable is a repository report, standalone figures and an executed notebook companion. It is not a hosted interactive Data app or a published site.
