International Education
Alberta Education continued to embrace the diversity of the province’s population and support initiatives 
that promote an understanding and respect of connections and differences between the world’s people. The 
ministry contributes to building a globally skilled workforce by providing Alberta students with opportunities 
to develop global competencies and intercultural skills. 
In 2024-25, Alberta Education continued to administer student exchange programs (virtual and/or in-person) 
with Germany, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Quebec, Spain and Ukraine. Through in-person programs, Alberta 
students are paired with students from a region that speaks the language they are learning and alternate 
sharing their homes, schools and communities with their partner for two to three months at a time. In virtual 
programs, student partners participate in weekly online meetings over six weeks. Students take turns hosting 
meetings and discussing various topics. Both in-person and virtual exchanges provide valuable personal 
and academic experiences and provide valuable opportunities for developing intercultural awareness and 
appreciation.
Alberta Education signed a memorandum of understanding (MOU) with the state of Hesse, Germany on 
June 10, 2024. The MOU reaffirms the long-standing relationship for the education exchange program for 
students, teachers and school administrators, and promotes partnerships between schools in both jurisdictions 
based on mutual interests and shared goals in language learning, career and technology studies and other 
areas of common interest. The agreement will be used to formalize regular connections, strengthen existing 
partnerships and promote collaboration that will improve the education system of both jurisdictions. 
The MOU will be effective for five years, with an option for renewal. By collaborating with international 
jurisdictions to exchange knowledge about educational programming, Alberta’s government is better 
positioning students to achieve academic success while increasing their competitiveness to pursue post-
secondary studies and high-demand careers in Alberta’s job market.

Collaborate with other ministries to expand mental health and well-being supports for students.
Alberta Education continued to support school authorities in providing mental health supports and 
services that focus on prevention, promotion and early short-term intervention within the education 
system. Additionally, the ministry collaborated with other ministries, such as Health and Mental Health and 
Addiction, and partners, including Alberta Health Services and school authorities, to build an integrated 
pathway to support access to appropriate health-related services and supports at school and in the community. 
For example, a new specialized education program was established in 2024-25 in partnership with The Red 
Deer School Division to support Recovery Alberta’s Step Up Step Down program. This program supports 
youth aged 13 to 17 who have significant addiction and mental health concerns and ensures that they have 
access to an education program while they receive treatment.
Mental Health Supports
Alberta Education continued to implement programs and services to support student mental health. In 2024-
25, the ministry collaborated with Mental Health and Addiction to enhance the integration of child and 
youth recovery-oriented addiction and mental health supports in schools and communities. 
Mental Health in Schools Pilot 
Alberta Education continued the Mental Health in Schools pilot project to improve the delivery of mental 
health supports and services for students and provision of tools, training and resources for the school 
community. In 2024-25, the ministry provided $20 million to support 79 two-year pilot projects in school 
authorities across the province. This included funding to extend 59 of those Mental Health in Schools pilots 
that were initially set to end in December 2024, to continue until June 2025.

In 2024, the Learning Disruption Funding was renamed the Literacy and Numeracy Support Funding and 
expanded to include Kindergarten children. Through the grant, $10 million was made available to school 
authorities for 2024-25 to support the literacy and numeracy skill development for students identified as 
requiring extra support through the screening assessments.
In March 2025, Alberta Education announced an additional $7.5 million of support funding for 2024-25 to 
ensure that schools have the necessary resources and staff to provide interventions for students in Kindergarten 
to Grade 3, helping to prevent any student from falling behind in reading and math.
Student Evaluation and Assessments
Student evaluation and assessment is an integral part of the teaching and learning process. Assessment 
information is used to improve student learning, enhance instruction to students and ensure that student 
learning needs are met. While no individual assessment can provide a complete picture of a student’s knowledge 
and skills, standardized assessments such as diploma examinations and provincial achievement tests provide 
consistent measures of student achievement.
In 2024-25, Alberta Education continued to develop, administer and mark provincial achievement tests and 
diploma examinations to report student achievement, ensure standards are maintained and assist schools, school 
authorities and the province in monitoring and improving student learning.
Although the implementation of new curriculum in English Language Arts and Literature and Math for 
Grade 6 students became mandatory in the 2023-24 school year, Grade 6 provincial achievement tests in 
English Language Arts and Literature and Math were not administered. This gave teachers and students an 
additional year to become familiar with the new curriculum. Instead, more than 59,000 field tests in these 
subjects were administered between April and June 2024. The results demonstrated that students achieved 
reasonable proficiency in the tested concepts. Alberta Education will administer Grade 6 provincial achievement 
tests in English Language Arts and Literature and Math in 2024-25. 
Similarly, Grade 6 provincial achievement tests for the first year of implementation of the new curricula in 
French First Language and Literature, French Immersion Language Arts and Literature and Science will not 
be administered in 2024-25; field testing of updated assessments occurred instead. 
Alberta Education continued transitioning to a new digital platform for provincial assessments. The transition to 
digital assessments is being implemented through an incremental process to allow teachers and students time to 
learn and adjust. The 2024-25 school year was the second year of optional implementation of Humanities Part 
A: Written Response diploma examinations and provincial achievement tests on the digital assessment platform. 
Nearly all school authorities are now administering either provincial assessments or field tests using 
the platform. 
Canadian Adult Education Credential 
Earning a high school diploma or equivalency credential is a key education milestone that provides the 
foundation for future education, career opportunities and personal success. In May 2024, Alberta successfully 
transitioned to the Canadian Adult Education Credential (CAEC) from the General Educational Development 
(GED) program. The CAEC project began in 2019 under the Council of Ministers of Education, Canada. 
Alberta Education led the development of the CAEC high school equivalency credential, working with other 
jurisdictions to develop a fair, reliable and credible assessment that accurately reflects the skills and competencies 
that employers and post-secondary institutions would expect from a Canadian senior high school equivalency 
credential. Like the GED, the CAEC is for adults who do not have a high school diploma but need a credential 
to open new doors to education or employment opportunities. The CAEC comprises five subject-area tests 
in French or English: reading, writing, math, science and social studies. Eight provinces and territories have 
signed agreements with Alberta to deliver the assessments. In 2024-25, approximately 3,500 CAEC exams were 
administered in Alberta.

Performance Measure 1. a:
High School Completion
Rationale
•
High school completion is important for entry to the labour force and post-secondary programs.
While most students complete high school within three years of entering Grade 10, the five-year rate
recognizes that it may take more time for some students to finish high school.
•
Alberta’s high school completion rate includes students who earn a high school diploma or certificate
of high school achievement and students who enter an Alberta post-secondary institution or an
apprenticeship program within five years of entering Grade 10. Another group comprises those who
have earned credits in a minimum of five Grade 12 courses, including one Language Arts diploma
examination course and three other diploma examination courses.
Target
•
The target was not met.
Discussion
•
In 2023-24, 87.1 per cent of students completed high school within five years of entering Grade 10.
•
The high school completion rate has increased over time by 0.9 per cent, from 86.2 per cent in 2019-20
to 87.1 per cent in 2023-24 (Chart 1.a).
•
Participation in diploma examinations was impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic. Diploma examinations
were cancelled in April and June of 2020, and participation was optional during the 2020-21 school year.
During this period, students who did not write diploma examinations received an exemption and their
official mark was based solely on their school-awarded mark. In 2021-22, the January administration of
diploma examinations was cancelled to support students affected by learning disruptions and the weighting
of diploma examinations was temporarily lowered from 30 per cent to 10 per cent. The changes to the
administration and weighting of diploma examinations may have affected the high school completion
rates and caution should be exercised when interpreting the trend over time.
•
The three-year high school completion rate is highly correlated with the five-year rate as most students
included in the five-year rate complete high school in three years. For the cohort that entered Grade
10 in 2019-20, 80.7 per cent of students completed high school within three years while 87.1 per cent
completed high school within five years (Table 1.a). This makes the three-year rate a reliable predictor of
the five-year rate in subsequent years. Projections indicate the five-year rate is likely to fluctuate over the
next few years.
•
Additionally, some students who do not complete high school in the Kindergarten to Grade 12 education
system do so as young adults in the adult learning system. In 2024, 94.9 per cent of Albertans aged 25 to
34 reported they had completed high school. This result has remained stable over time.

Endnote B: Technical Notes
Assessing Results Relative to Targets
Performance targets set expectations for results within specific time frames and are the basis for planning 
improvements and assessing results that are within the sphere of influence of the Ministry and school 
authorities. They are an important way to gauge whether the education system is improving or falling behind 
in obtaining desired results in key areas. Targets clearly establish desired levels of performance better than 
recent performance, to be attained within a specific time frame. Targets are quantifiable and expressed in 
numerical terms, such as percentages or ratios. They are used as a key tool to drive, measure, improve and 
manage performance.
For survey measures, performance is considered to have met the target if the confidence interval for the 
result includes the target value. For example, if a measure result is 77 per cent with a confidence interval 
of 77 per cent ± 2.5 per cent and a target of 79 per cent, then the target has been met, since the target value 
lies within the interval [74.5 per cent, 79.5 per cent]. If the result on the same measure is 75 per cent, with 
a confidence interval of 75 per cent ± 2.5 per cent, then the target has not been met since the target value of 
79 per cent does not lie within the interval [72.5 per cent, 77.5 per cent].
This method of assessing performance represents a high level of achievement in relation to the target and 
considers the sampling variability in the survey process.
For provincial assessments and student outcome measures (e.g., high school completion rate), a one sample 
Chi-square statistical test is used to determine whether the result is significantly different from the target. 
The target is met when results are not significantly different from the target. The magnitude of the difference 
required between the result and the target to establish significance depends on the sample size, for example, 
the number of students writing an exam.
Assessing Results Over Time
Changes in results over time are assessed in the discussion of each measure. For survey measures, which 
are based on probability sampling, confidence intervals are considered. See Use of Confidence Intervals 
for Interpreting Survey and Survey Measure Results in Endnote A on page 82, for an explanation of how 
confidence intervals are used to assess changes over time for survey measures.
Changes over time are assessed with a two sample Chi-square statistical test for measures based on tests of 
student learning or on administrative data, i.e., derived from tracking groups of students over time, such 
as dropout and high school completion rates. The test determines whether the difference between two 
proportions (e.g., base year and current year results) is significant. The calculation of Chi-square is based on 
the difference between what was observed and what would be expected assuming that the proportions were 
the same. Under a simple test of proportions, a Chi-square value of “1” represents a difference of one standard 
deviation, while a Chi-square value of 3.84 is significant at the five per cent level. Discussion of results over 
time for the non-survey measures is supported by this test of significant difference.
Trend Lines
Although not appearing in the annual report, trend lines are an aid for interpreting the results for measures 
reported in the annual report. Trend lines augment data interpretation techniques already in use, such as the 
calculation of confidence intervals, and are used to determine the significance of changes in measure results 
over time.

Attrition Adjustment
Attrition estimates are applied to all the four student outcomes measures – the High School Completion Rate, 
the Post-Secondary Transition Rate, the Dropout Rate and the Diploma Examination Participation Rate. 
Attrition is the estimate of the number of students from the Grade 10 cohort who leave the province or die in 
the years subsequent to the start of Grade 10. The attrition estimate is based on the following factors:
a)	mortality;
b)	emigration;
c)	interprovincial out migration;
d)	an estimate of the percentage of 16-, 17-, and 18-year-olds who are enrolled in school 
	
(school participation rate); and
e)	a downward adjustment to account for youth who are not in school.
The provincial attrition estimate aggregates census division level data from Alberta Demographic Estimates 
48_CD_DR_AG_2002_2024.xlsx.
Out-of-Province Post-Secondary Student Adjustment
Alberta Education does not specifically track Alberta students who participate in post-secondary programs 
outside of Alberta. An estimate of the number of students who have left the province to pursue post- 
secondary education is therefore factored into the high school to post-secondary transition rate.
The out-of-province estimate uses aggregate counts of Alberta students receiving financial assistance for 
studies both in-province and out-of-province based on data from Advanced Education’s Students Finance 
System (SFS) and the Data Collection and Reporting (DCAR) system. The premise is that in any given year, 
the percentage of students from the Grade 10 Cohort attending post-secondary programs out-of-province is 
similar to the percentage of students receiving financial assistance for study out-of-province.
Provincial Achievement Tests
2024-25 provincial achievement test results will be available in fall of 2025 and published in the Annual 
Report Update. 
Students in grades 6 and 9 write provincial achievement tests annually in English Language Arts and 
Literature, Mathematics, Science and Social Studies. The achievement test results for grades 6 and 9 provide 
information on how well Alberta students are meeting provincial standards in the core academic subjects. 
Tests are developed and administered by teachers and educators and are based on the provincial curriculum. 
Results are reported in relation to the acceptable standard and standard of excellence.
A student achieving the acceptable standard in a specific grade shows an adequate understanding of the core 
knowledge and adequate basic skills essential to that course. A student achieving the standard of excellence 
consistently shows a deeper understanding of the concepts of the course by demonstrating an ability to 
integrate information and evaluate it from various points of view.
For each achievement test, the cut scores for the acceptable standard and the standard of excellence are set 
initially by a standard-setting committee of about 20 teachers selected from across the province using the 
Modified Angoff and Bookmark Standard Setting Procedures. These procedures use teachers’ judgment to 
determine a minimum number of items a student must answer correctly – the cut score – to achieve each 
standard. In subsequent years, the cut scores for each test are adjusted through test equating. This ensures

Diploma Examinations
2024-25 diploma examination results will be available in fall 2025 and published in the Annual Report 
Update.
Examinations are administered in all diploma examination courses in January, June and August each year. 
Examinations are administered in selected courses in November and April. Provincial assessments are meant 
to complement but not replace day-to-day classroom assessment. Results on diploma examinations show how 
well Alberta students in public, separate, francophone, charter and accredited independent (private) schools 
are meeting provincial expectations as outlined in the Programs of Study. Examination items are developed 
and standards are established by committees of teachers. The examination design is vetted through committees 
of stakeholders and by a standard-setting committee of teachers. 
A test equating initiative has been phased in for the diploma examination program so that over time 
examinations are consistent and the results are comparable. The multiple-choice portion of the examinations 
contains a set of items common to administrations in subsequent years. By comparing the achievement of 
students writing in a baseline session with those writing in any subsequent administration on the common 
items and on the unique items, Alberta Education is able to determine whether or not the examinations are 
of equal difficulty. Student scores on the subsequent examinations can then be equated back to the baseline 
examinations to remove any influence that differences in examination difficulty may have on student scores.
Through equating, test results for a particular course are based on an identical standard and, consequently, can 
be directly compared over time. As a result of implementing the equating process, diploma examination results 
are comparable over time as follows:

Refunds or commuted value transfers are available when a teacher ceases employment, subject to certain restrictions.

form of a lump sum payment or a survivor pension.

refunded service is allowed on a basis that is cost neutral to the Plans.
