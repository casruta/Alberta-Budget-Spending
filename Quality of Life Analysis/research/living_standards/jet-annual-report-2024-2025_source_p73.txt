This measure indicates whether the province is meeting supply and demand pressures for child 
care and is directly related to population growth, policy changes and any government commitments 
to expanding child care programs. 
In 2024-25, the number of licensed child care spaces increased by ten per cent, relative to the 
previous year, increasing one percentage point and exceeding the target of nine per cent. These 
results are in alignment with the targets set out in the CACWELCC agreement and action plan.   
This increase is influenced by operators meeting increased demand for child care throughout the 
province as out-of-pocket costs to parents continue to decrease through implementation of the 
Canada Alberta Early Learning Child Care Agreement. Space creation efforts implemented by the 
provincial government have also contributed to this increase and the result may be further 
influenced by population trends (including growth and interprovincial migration), labour market 
participation and personal choice. 
There are several factors that continue to impact space creation, such as construction delays, delays 
in applicants collecting approval and collateral and the availability of certified Early Childhood 
Educators (ECEs) to staff new or expanded facilities.
