64 Jobs, Economy and Trade | Annual Report 2024–2025 
Increasing Affordable Child Care Spaces  
Under the CACWELCC Agreement, Alberta committed to creating up to 68,700 child care spaces for 
children up to kindergarten age, including at least 42,500 non-profit spaces and up to 26,200 
private spaces. As part of the 2023-2026 CACWELCC Action Plan, the timeline to open child care 
spaces was extended by one year from March 2026 to March 2027 in recognition of the time it takes 
to create new child care spaces.  
As of March 31, 2025, 142,700 licensed child care spaces were available across the province for 
children up to kindergarten age, including 58,800 non-profit spaces and 83,900 private spaces. In 
2024-25, 14,300 net new licensed spaces were created, an increase of 11 per cent compared to 
2023-24. 
In 2024-25, the department continued to support the creation of licensed child care spaces through 
the Space Creation Grant Program, providing 108 grants totaling $31.8 million to develop 
approximately 8,000 new spaces. Since the program launched in 2022, a total of 242 grant 
recipients have received $68.1 million, resulting in approximately 16,900 planned spaces, with 
10,600 already established. 
 
Alberta’s Space Creation Grant played a key role in supporting the development of new, affordable 
spaces. However, following the announcement of $53 million in additional federal funding through 
the Early Learning and Child Care Infrastructure Fund in spring 2024, the department introduced a 
renewed approach focused on public and non-profit providers. 
 
In 2024-25, the Space Creation Grant intake ended and the Building Blocks Capital Grant Program 
launched in January 2025. With a total investment of up to $53 million, this program offers 
one-time capital funding to support the creation of full-time licensed early learning and child care 
spaces in non-profit and public facility-based programs. It features two funding streams—Major 
Capital and Minor Capital Grants—and prioritizes projects in hig h-need areas. The program’s first 
intake received 72 applications, requesting nearly double the available funding, highlighting strong 
sector demand and interest. 
 
Additionally, the Child Care Community Needs Assessment initiative was launched in January 2025 
to better understand the unique child care demands of diverse and vulnerable communities. This 
collaboration with municipalities, First Nations and Metis Settlements aims to identify gaps in child 
care access and inform community-driven solutions for more inclusive care options. 
Family Day Home Modernization 
The Government of Alberta introduced new contracts for licensed family day home agencies on 
January 1, 2025, to offer a consistent and transparent funding model to agencies that oversee family 
day home programs in the province. In 2024-25, all 86 Family Day Home Agency Contracts were 
signed. As of March 31, 2025, there are approximately 18,700 spaces and 3,500 educators in 
home-based child care in Alberta. This is an increase of 1,400 spaces and increase of 260 educators 
compared to 2023-24.  
Enhancements to family day home monitoring were also made to ensure adherence to contract 
requirements and alignment with provincial legislation and standards. Increased monitoring also 
helps to prevent fraud, promote accountability, ensure the integrity of financial information and 
improve operational efficiency.
