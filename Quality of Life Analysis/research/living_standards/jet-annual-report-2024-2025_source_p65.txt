 
 Results Analysis 
 
 
Jobs, Economy and Trade | Annual Report 2024-2025 63 
Since the signing of the CACWELCC Agreement in November 2021, child care fees for families with 
children up to kindergarten age in licensed spaces were reduced by an average of 50 per cent in 
January 2022, with further reductions to an average of $15 per day in January 2024— saving 
families approximately $750 per month or $9,000 per year per child, regardless of income.  
In 2024-25, $797.2 million was provided to licensed child care providers through Affordability 
Grants to reduce child care fees for 129,000 children up to kindergarten age, enrolled in child care 
spaces in daycare facilities, family day homes and preschools. Additionally, in 2024-25, $84.2 
million in Cost Increase Replacement Funding was provided to programs receiving the Affordability 
Grant to help providers manage rising operational costs without passing them on to families.  
The department also supported families with children up to kindergarten age by providing a child 
care subsidy for families with an annual household income of less than $180,000, further reducing 
fees. In 2024-25, $194 million in subsidies was provided. As of March 31, 2025, 67,500 children 
were receiving some level of subsidy, making child care more affordable for families. 
Alberta’s government recognizes the importance of out-of-school care programs. In 2024-25, $56.8 
million in subsidies were provided to school-aged children in households with an annual income of 
less than $90,000. As of March 31, 2025, approximately 13,000 school-aged children received some 
level of subsidy. 
Further, the subsidy rate for out-of-school care increased from $366 to $644 during July and August 
2024 to help families offset the higher costs of full-time child care during the summer months. 
The department continued to offer the Infant Care Incentive program to support affordability for 
families with infants. The program incentivizes licensed child care providers to offer child care 
spaces for infants up to 18 months of age by offering providers $150 per month for infants 
receiving care. The program provided a total of $15.42 million to licensed child care providers in 
2024-25. 
In July 2024, nearly 2,000 facility-based operators, family day home educators, family day home 
agencies and preschools completed the Early Learning and Child Care Cost Questionnaire which 
aimed to gather a better understanding the costs to deliver high-quality child care for children up to 
kindergarten age. The questionnaire informed the development of an early learning and child care 
funding approach and continued to support ongoing discussions with the federal government on 
Alberta's unique child care system. 
In January 2025, Alberta’s government announced that as of April 1, 2025, it would implement a 
new flat monthly parent fee of $326.25 for full-time care; equivalent to $15 per day. This, in 
addition to affordability grants and child care subsidies, reduced child care fees to make 
high-quality, inclusive and affordable child care more accessible. 
Payments to Child Care Providers 
The department continued to provide claim advance payments to child care providers, beginning in 
March 2024, enabling them to receive grant payments sooner. Child care programs receive 
approximately 80 per cent of their monthly claim at the beginning of each month to reduce financial 
pressures on providers.
