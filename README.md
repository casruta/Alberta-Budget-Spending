# Alberta's budget: more dollars, uneven results

**Prepared by:** Kacper Ruta · **Evidence reviewed:** October 9, 2026

## Executive summary

- **Resources:** From 2018–19 to 2024–25, program expense rose **30.9%** and capital spending **19.6%**. After population and consumer-price adjustments, spending per resident fell **4.3%** and **12.6%**, respectively.
- **Results:** Surgical access and some ambulance response times improved. Emergency waits lengthened; education measures and housing-support outputs missed targets. Poverty rose on the current measure, while life expectancy recovered from its pandemic-era low.
- **Interpretation:** The evidence shows uneven performance. Spending, completed projects and social outcomes answer different questions; these data cannot isolate a government's effect or establish whether services met population needs.

**Scope:** Financial actuals cover fiscal 2015–16 to 2024–25; each outcome below has its own cutoff. Amounts are Canadian dollars. **CPI-adjusted** means adjusted for broad consumer prices, not sector-specific costs. Seven key charts lead the report; expand supporting evidence to see all **21 reviewed figures**, plus **21 original archive figures**.

## Spending

Population grew **13.9%** and consumer prices **20.1%** from 2018 to 2024. Together they increased the population–price benchmark **36.8%**, exceeding program expense growth. This adjustment measures spending intensity, rather than service volume or quality.

![Program spending in nominal dollars and CPI-adjusted dollars per resident, showing that their trends differ.](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/figures/01_spending_intensity.png)

| Function | 2024–25 actual | Nominal change since 2018–19 | CPI-adjusted change per resident |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Health | $29.560B | +34.8% | −1.4% |
| Basic and advanced education | $17.197B | +15.8% | −15.3% |
| Social services | $8.462B | +44.2% | +5.4% |
| Other programs | $16.118B | +35.8% | −0.7% |
| **Total programs** | **$71.337B** | **+30.9%** | **−4.3%** |

Education combines basic and advanced education **per total resident**, not K–12 spending per pupil. These functional totals differ from ministry operating expense. Accounting changes limit comparability. [Source: Final Results, PDF pp. 13–14](Budget%20PDFs/2024-2025%20Budget.pdf#page=13)

## Infrastructure

Capital spending recovered to **$7.243B in 2024–25**, up 15.0% over the prior year. Adjusted spending per resident remained **12.6% below 2018–19**, but only **1.4% below 2019–20**: the baseline matters.

![Nominal and CPI-adjusted Capital Plan dollars per resident over ten fiscal observations.](Infrastructure%20Analysis/plots/02_spending_per_resident.png)

Municipal and health envelopes accounted for **85.6% of the latest $943M net increase**. Actual spending was **12.7% below the paired budget**; scheduling changes can explain underspending without cancellation. The Misericordia emergency department opened in November 2023 and Calgary's cancer centre became fully operational in October 2024. Selected openings establish delivery, but gross capacities do not establish net, staffed capacity per resident. [Evidence and sources](Infrastructure%20Analysis/REPORT.md)

## Health

**Emergency and imaging access worsened:** from 2021–22 to 2024–25, the emergency wait measure rose **4.5 → 7.0 hours**, while MRI shares within priority targets fell **68% → 41%** and CT **87% → 80%**. The emergency measure is the **90th percentile at the 16 largest sites**, not a provincial average.

![Emergency-department 90th-percentile waits rose from 4.5 to 7.0 hours, while MRI and CT shares within clinical priority targets declined.](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/figures/08_emergency_and_imaging.png)

**Other results were mixed.** Completed hip, knee and cataract cases within benchmarks improved **62.4% → 72.8%**, **53.3% → 62.5%** and **59.7% → 62.8%** between 2023–24 and 2024–25; patients still waiting are excluded. Metro ambulance response times worsened **13.8 → 14.2 minutes** at the 90th percentile, while rural and remote times improved. Life expectancy recovered to **81.53 years in 2024**, still 0.43 years below 2019; calendar 2023 and 2024 are preliminary. Eligible first mental-health/addiction emergency visits without prior public-service contact were **19.7% against a 17.9% target in 2023–24**; this measures contact, not recovery. [Health sources and definitions](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/REPORT.md)

## Education

Five-year completion was **87.1% in school year 2023–24**, above 2019–20's 86.2% but below its peak and **88.8% target**. Self-identified First Nations, Métis and Inuit completion was **69.7%**, below its 71.5% target; the overall series includes these students.

In 2024–25, Grade 9 language arts and mathematics results were **69.7% and 51.7%**, below **72.0% and 55.3% targets**. Participation and assessment disruptions affect comparisons; these weighted, enrolment-based results do not measure population literacy. [Education evidence](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/REPORT.md)

## Living standards

Average weekly earnings rose **$1,148 → $1,328 from 2018 to 2024**, but fell **3.7% after CPI adjustment**. Unemployment was **7.0% in 2024**, versus 6.5% in 2018. Earnings reflect employment mix and hours, rather than median disposable household income.

On the current **2023-base Market Basket Measure**, Alberta's poverty rate rose **10.2% → 11.0% from 2023 to 2024**. Canada also rounded to 11.0%; matching estimates does not establish statistical equivalence. The chart keeps the older poverty base separate. [Poverty source](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1110013501)

![Alberta and Canadian poverty rates shown in separate panels for the 2018-base and 2023-base Market Basket Measures.](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/figures/11_poverty.png)

## Housing and access

Housing-support output fell **2,302 → 798 from 2023–24 to 2024–25**, against a 1,500 target. The latest result combines **388 units and 410 net additional rent-supported households**, includes regenerated units and measures neither newly built homes alone nor net housing stock.

![Combined affordable housing units and additional rent-supported households fell to 798 in 2024–25, below a target of 1,500.](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/figures/10_housing_support.png)

Other access measures add context: continuing-care placement within the benchmark was **63.0%**, preliminary and below its 66% target, among admitted clients. AISH medical adjudication took **5.3 weeks**, within its nine-week target but longer than the prior year's 4.3 weeks, after complete documentation. Childcare fees averaged **$15/day in January 2024** in the licensed up-to-kindergarten scope; **142.7k licensed spaces** were reported by March 31, 2025, including 14.3k net new spaces. Licensed capacity is not attendance or staffed availability. [Sources and scope](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/REPORT.md)

## Safety

From calendar 2019 to 2023, police-reported violent crime rose **1,462 → 1,591 incidents per 100,000 people (+8.8%)**; property crime fell **5,894 → 4,752 (−19.4%)**. These reported rates exclude unreported victimization. Rural/urban categories follow police services, and the panels have independent scales. [Source: PDF p. 28; methods p. 44](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/research/source_verification/downloads/pses-annual-report-2024-2025.pdf#page=28)

![Separate violent- and property-crime rate panels for Alberta, with urban and rural police-service comparisons.](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/figures/04_safety.png)

## What remains uncertain

A consistent **net, staffed capacity** series, sector-specific costs, complete waiting lists and subgroup outcomes are missing. Housing-cost burden, food insecurity, life satisfaction and healthy life expectancy are also unresolved. These gaps prevent an overall quality-of-life score, a needs-based adequacy judgment or causal attribution to government policy.

## Sources and methods

The reviewed financial source is **2024–25 Final Results** (the local filename says “Budget”). July population and calendar CPI are proxies for fiscal-year conditions. Outcome dates above are separate from the financial cutoff.

<details>
<summary>Calculation, primary sources and reconciliation</summary>

```text
Change in CPI-adjusted spending per resident
    = (endpoint spending / starting spending)
      / (endpoint population / starting population)
      / (endpoint CPI / starting CPI) - 1
```

CPI is compounded from rounded Alberta annual rates. Financial inputs use one report vintage; original budget/actual pairs retain their contemporary presentation. Accounting-policy changes in 2019–20 and 2021–22 still limit strict comparability.

- [Final Results PDF, pp. 13–17](Budget%20PDFs/2024-2025%20Budget.pdf#page=13): spending, population, prices and earnings.
- [Official ministry PDF inventory](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/research/source_verification/current_pdf_inventory.csv): source URLs and hashes.
- [Statistics Canada poverty table 11-10-0135-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1110013501), released April 29, 2026: separate MBM bases.
- [Life table 13-10-0837-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1310083701), released January 13, 2026: period life expectancy and uncertainty.
- [Financial reconciliation](Quality%20of%20Life%20Analysis/review/current_website_functional_all80_comparison.csv): all 80 selected cells matched the current official download. The source's $6M component-to-total residual in 2022–23 is retained.

Detailed reports contain metric definitions, source pages, raw inputs and remaining evidence gaps.
