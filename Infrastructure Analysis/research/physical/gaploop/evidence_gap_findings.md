# Additional physical and service indicators recovered in the evidence-gap loop

These additions use only local official PDF sources. No network retries or external-memory facts were used. The original physical research artifacts are preserved. Exact excerpts and one-indexed PDF pages appear in `additional_indicators.csv`/JSON and `source_pages.txt`; health subagent findings and full source pages are in `health/`.

## Useful additions to a report

**Pre 2019 school capacity:** the 2017–18 Measuring Up outcome overview, physical PDF p. 93, says approximately **21, 600 new student spaces** were created and **15, 000 spaces modernized or replaced**, in the context of students entering new/updated schools in September 2017. This supplies a concrete predecessor-period physical capacity observation, beyond cumulative project-count pipelines. The overview does not provide a reconciled net seat inventory, a list of predecessor closures, or a fully standardized output metric comparable with later annual-report tables. State this as what the overview reports; do not turn 21, 600 versus 5, 000+ into a political productivity ratio.

The 2016–17 government message, physical PDF p. 3, states **83 new-school and modernization projects completed** during the fiscal-year achievements discussion. That is a mixed project measure, not 83 new schools, and not automatically comparable to later project lists containing planning/design work. Its existence means it is too strong to say every pre 2019 source gives only cumulative school counts; qualify that description as applying to the capital-plan pipeline measures already cited.

**Continuing care:**2018–19 p. 10 records ten ASLIprojects adding **1, 177 long-term-care spaces**.2022–23 p. 15 expressly reports completion of **871 new continuing-care spaces**. “Long-term-care” versus broader “continuing-care” and differences in program scope prevent a homogeneous annual series or netprovincialcapacity comparison. These are delivered/gross source claims, not staffed acute beds. The health note explains service-access changes and their denominator/policy confounds.

**Targeted road expansion:**2023–24 p. 23 says construction complete for **3.5 km of Highway 19 twinning**, from Highway 60 to 3.5 km east, with intersection/service-road works. This is route distance, not 3.5 lane-km or provincewide net growth.

**Public safety:**2023–24 p. 19 classifies Red Deer Justice Centre completed, andp 23 describes **12 courtrooms** plus space for future expansion. This is gross new-facility capacity; predecessor courtroom retirements are unquantified. Four courthouse projects still underway 2024–25 are not delivered facilities.

**Housing:**2024–25 p. 23 says Conklin construction complete for **five four-bedroom units and ten two/three-bedroom units**, or 15 units total. These are units, not 15 buildings or a provincial net-stock gain.

**Utilities:**2021–22 p. 15 expressly says Northern Lights Gas Co-op supply-line completion for Mackenzie County, approximately **100 km naturalgas pipeline constructed**; p. 23 gives the same physical corridor description. This is a local utility-service expansion, not naturalgas production capacity or a count of newly served households.

**Digital service infrastructure:**2020–21 p. 23 gives completed One IMTEnterprise ITEnvironment consolidation of **37 independently managed data centres into three enterprise-class centres**. The motor-vehicle MOVES system upgrade is also completed in that section. Fewer centres can reflect consolidation and improved service rather than reduced capacity. Neither is proof of broadband connections, digital uptake or quantified service availability. The latest$48 million Broadband Strategy remains a financial input without verified connection output in the local consolidated corpus.

## Performance indicators worth keeping separate from physical stock

2017–18 p. 107 gives government-owned/operated building energy intensity **1, 655 MJ/m²2014–15; 1, 6642015–16; 1, 6512016–17; 1, 6492017–18**. The p. 140 methodology states weather normalization was strengthened 2016–17, with historical results/targets revised for comparability. A same-vintage intensity improvement is an energy-performance observation, not evidence of less deferred maintenance, more service capacity or improved provincewide asset condition. No corresponding consistent latest-year series was located in the abbreviated 2020–25 source set.

The health subagent recovered continuingcare access **60%2015–16; 56%2016–17; 52%2017–18** within 30 days. The denominator is clients who moved in/admitted during the reporting period, not everyone waiting. Changing placement policy/preferences, delayed openings and water-damaged facilities constrain attribution. This does not provide a consistent decade-long net capacity series or isolate infrastructure from operational management.

## Remaining gaps

No full ten-year reconciled series of net school seats, staffed acute/continuing-care beds, retired buildings/bridges, water treatment capacity or licensed connections, municipal transit openings, eligible-household housing capacity, or broadband connections is supported by these local consolidated sources. Annual performance appendices are present in older source files but largely absent in the short 2020–25 year-end PDFs; absence from those reports is not evidence that a metric ceased to exist elsewhere. Departmental, institutional, municipal and CIHI/Statistics Canada sources remain needed. Zero filling or generic population scaling would manufacture an answer these sources do not establish.

## Final added observations

2019–20 p. 17 gives **810 km road rehabilitated**, preceding 2020–21's 830 km. Combine with earlier 860 km/1240 km only as separate reported maintenance observations with missing years and an unresolved route-versus-lane reporting basis; no decade trend is supported.

University of Lethbridge Destination has an explicitly dated **January 2020 completion** in 2019–20 p. 19; the description calls it replacement of an outdated, overcrowded sciencebuilding, without numerical net seats. University of Calgary MacKimmie redevelopment has completed status 2022–23 p. 19/p. 23 and a qualitative increase in student capacity. These improve post-secondary delivery coverage beyond the latest future NAIT/Mac Ewan examples.

Southwest Calgary Ring 31 km, Bragg Creekfloodworks and the 240 km Alberta Carbon Trunk Line are additional completed-status descriptions in 2021–22 p. 19/p. 23. The carbonpipeline is publiclygrant-supported private industrial infrastructure, so keep its ownership/scope distinct from provincial asset accounts and do not infer its actual first operating year from the 2021–22 list.
