# Evidence-gap loop: continuing care delivery and access

All page references below are **physical PDF pages, one-indexed**, computed from pdftotext form-feed page separators. No network requests were used in this loop.

## Explicit physical additions

- **2018–19: 1,177 long-term care spaces.** Official 2018–19 annual report (repository filename `2018-19 Budget.pdf`), PDF page 10, explicitly states completed construction on ten Affordable Supportive Living Initiative projects “adding 1,177 long term care spaces.” This is actual construction delivery, rather than the earlier 2,000-space policy goal. It does not document retirements, licensing, staffing, or full provincial net stock growth.
- **2022–23: 871 new continuing care spaces.** Official 2022–23 annual report (`2022-2023 Budget.pdf`), PDF page 15, states capital-plan delivery included completion of “871 new continuing care spaces,” alongside completed schools/roads/housing. The statement establishes delivered gross new spaces in the reported programme context; it does not specify supportive-living versus long-term-care composition, closures, replacement effects, or the full provincial stock.

These are differently labelled scopes, not a comparable annual provincial bed series. Do not interpret 871 versus 1,177 as a decline in total care stock or calculate growth between them. Both observations can be shown separately in a delivery evidence table with scope labels. Net provincial per-capita capacity remains unquantified.

## Access indicators and confounding

The continuing-care access measure is the share of **clients admitted during the reporting period** who moved in within 30 days of assessed-and-approved date. It is **not** the share of all waitlisted clients served within 30 days (methodology: 2017–18 report, PDF page 147).

Results: 60% in 2015–16 (2015–16 report PDF pages 104–105), 56% in 2016–17 (2016–17 report PDF page 128), and 52% in 2017–18 (2017–18 report PDF page 122). The two-year change is −8 percentage points. The 2016–17 target was 62%; the 2017–18 target 65%. These are access/service indicators, not facility capacity counts.

The 2015–16 discussion explains a new May 2015 access/waitlist policy giving greater weight to individual needs and preferences. It says preference for particular facilities may extend assessment-to-admission time. The 2016–17 report attributes challenges to curtailed planned home-care investment, spaces not opening as planned, and major water incidents requiring restoration, displacement and repatriation; delayed spaces were planned for 2017–18. The 2017–18 discussion describes rising demand and gradual expansion as the planned 2,000 spaces begin to become available, but gives no annual actual count in that passage. Do not treat 2,000 as delivered or attribute access deterioration solely to infrastructure spending.

## What remains absent

The available 2020–25 full reports/selected year-end documents inspected do not establish a matching continuing-care 30-day access series here. Recent specific project/planned bed counts cannot be joined to these access percentages as a causal before/after estimate. An AHS comparable actual staffed/licensed continuing-care stock and access series, with policy definitions and age-adjusted denominators, would be needed. General population is a spending denominator; seniors and assessed demand are more relevant for care-capacity adequacy.
