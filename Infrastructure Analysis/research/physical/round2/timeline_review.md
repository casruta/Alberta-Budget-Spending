# Project timeline and explicit capacity review

Outputs: `project_timeline.csv`/JSON (17 named project observations), `explicit_capacity_observations.csv` (5 aggregate observations), and `timeline_source_pages.txt` (14 full source pages for checking headings and nearby context).

All page numbers are one-indexed physical PDF pages. Empty CSV fields / JSON null mean unknown or unverified, never zero. `first_observed_earlier_stage_year` is the earliest stage located in the scoped local reports during this review; it is NOT necessarily the project announcement, groundbreaking, or initiation year. Values are fiscal-year labels. `operational_year_if_verified` is deliberately null across this table because the retrieved sources do not establish exact independent calendar opening years; status describes when a report says a facility was operational/open. A reported expected opening date is not actual evidence.

## Trustworthy quantitative physical observations

- **2018–19 baseline:** ten completed Affordable Supportive Living Initiative projects described as adding **1,177 long-term-care spaces** (2018–19 PDF p10). These are reported additions for that project set, not a reconciled provincial net-bed change.
- **2021–22 schools:** **5,000+ new** and **5,900+ modernized** student spaces separately (PDF p20). New spaces are gross delivered capacity; without retired/replaced seats a provincial net figure remains unknown.
- **2024–25 schools:** **11,000+ new and modernized** combined spaces (PDF p20). This differs from a new-only measure and should not be shown as equivalent growth.
- **High Prairie renal dialysis:** introduction of service with **six stations** in the completed-project section (2021–22 PDF p23). This is a verified local service-capacity addition. Provincewide net station growth is unknown.
- **Misericordia ED:** completed design capacity around **60,000 visits/year** (2023–24 PDF p23). This is not actual throughput or a bed measure. The prior year's “additional35,000 visits/year” appears in an ongoing/expected project description (2022–23 PDF p23), so do not promote a planned increment into observed realized growth.
- **Gunn recovery:** purpose-built **100 treatment beds**, construction complete in 2023–24 (p23); 2024–25 explicitly says Red Deer, Lethbridge, Gunn are open, with **200 treatment beds combined** (p15). Gunn must not be added again to200. These are addiction treatment beds, not acute care beds.
- **Bow River highway bridge:** westbound widening **three lanes to four** (2024–25 p23) provides one explicit additional local lane. New eastbound bridge exists in the description but its lane count is unspecified.
- **Grande Prairie bypass:** **12 km of four-lane highway** constructed (2019–20 p19); final-stage paving later completed2022–23 (p23). Do not double-count corridor length across its stages.
- **Calgary ring:** **101 km entire ring** complete (2023–24 p23). This is the whole connected corridor, not101km of construction newly undertaken2023–24.

No record converts design visits into staffed capacity, adds addiction beds to acute-care beds, imputes seats from building names, or claims project capital cost is a physical output.

## Completion-definition change

2020–21 and2021–22 PDF p23: “Projects are defined as completed when they are operational” and closeout funding may continue. 2022–23,2023–24,2024–25 PDF p23: “Projects are defined as completed when construction has ended” and preoperational funding may continue. This affects interpretations of delivery timing. The source's broad operational definition is not independent proof of a exact calendar opening, especially phase-based assets. The timeline records the declaration without fabricating dates.

The full PDF-page extracts contain section headings needed to verify that named descriptions sit under completed/ongoing rather than relying solely on a keyword match. The broad yearly highlights have a2024–25 health count contradiction documented in the initial narrative; timeline uses named granular entries.

## Known missingness

School named project preceding stages are largely unavailable in this consolidated-report set; blanks are expected. Institutional/municipal annual reports, actual opening records, retired-asset/seat counts, staffing, and utilization would be required to complete a consistent net-stock or service-adequacy assessment. The selected timeline is representative evidence, not an exhaustive inventory of every provincial project.
