# Health infrastructure: delivered assets versus plans

Source-backed findings use the Government of Alberta annual reports present in the repository. Despite filenames containing “Budget,” their internal headings identify these as Final Results / Year-End Reports. Evidence is valid through March 31, 2025; it does not establish service openings after that date. Project-level examples are selective rather than a complete inventory.

The UCP took office in April 2019. Hospitals delivered during its tenure are not automatically projects conceived or funded entirely by that government. The 2015–16 annual report (printed pages 16 and 94) already documents work on Grande Prairie and planning for Calgary Cancer Centre; the 2019–20 year-end report (printed page 16) describes those as continuing major projects. Attribution should distinguish project initiation, continued funding, construction completion, and operational opening.

## Verified physical changes

- **Grande Prairie Regional Hospital phases:** the 2020–21 annual report, printed page 23, lists **Phase 1** under completed projects, while **Phase 2** remains ongoing and is described as fitting out shelled space for a Mental Health and Addictions wing and a 28-bed medical-surgical inpatient wing. The 2021–22 annual report, printed page 23, separately lists **Phase 2** as completed. Both reports display $850 million covering **phases 1 and 2 jointly**; these are not two $850 million projects. Their completed-project definitions say “operational,” unlike the later reports' construction-ended definition. That classification is evidence of the reports' phase status, not independently verified timing of the whole hospital's patient opening. The project predates UCP. Neither the phase 2 planned 28 beds nor the gross new-hospital capacity establishes net provincial bed growth without predecessor/replacement capacity and staffing data.
- **Calgary Cancer Centre:** the 2022–23 annual report, printed page 23, puts construction in completed projects, capital plan funding $1.4 billion, and says the centre is anticipated to open to the public in 2024. Construction completed in 2022–23 must not be described as patients treated beginning that year. A replacement cancer centre's gross capacity also must not be treated as entirely net additional capacity.
- **Misericordia emergency department:** the 2023–24 annual report, printed page 23, confirms a completed emergency department with design capacity for around 60,000 patient visits per year ($85 million capital plan funding). This is design throughput, not actual visits or acute beds, and the source gives no prior throughput denominator.
- **Lakeview Recovery Community, Gunn:** the 2023–24 report, printed page 23, confirms construction of a 100-bed addiction treatment facility ($22 million commitment as of Budget 2023). By March 31, 2025, three recovery communities at Red Deer, Lethbridge and Gunn are open/existing with a combined 200 treatment beds (2024–25 report, printed page 15 explicitly says open; printed page 20 lists existing stock). The eight communities and 500+ treatment beds still in progress are future capacity. The Gunn 100 beds are already inside the 200 total.
- **Rockyview General ICU/CCU/GI redevelopment:** the 2024–25 annual report, printed page 23, confirms construction complete ($84 million), increased single-patient ICU and CCU units, and expanded GI clinic. No numeric net bed change is reported in this passage.
- **Chinook Regional Hospital, Lethbridge:** the 2024–25 annual report, printed page 23, confirms expansion of two operating rooms and more surgical inpatient rooms ($15 million). The wording supports two rooms expanded; it does not prove two net new rooms or a quantified net bed increase.

## Coverage and comparisons

Annual completion counts are **projects**, not new hospitals: 2021–22 reports six health facilities projects and capital programs completed, explicitly comprising two projects and four programs (printed page 20); 2022–23 reports four projects, 2023–24 five projects, and 2024–25 two projects. The program/project mix changes, preventing a naive homogeneous count series. The 2024–25 report records 17 health facility projects and 11 programs in progress; these are not delivered capacity.

The reported health building stock is “900+” owned or leased buildings in 2021–22, 2022–23, 2023–24 and 2024–25. It includes health, continuing-care and community-care buildings. This coarse lower-bound stock cannot establish zero building growth or an exact per-capita decline. It cannot be converted to an acute hospital count.

For population-adjusted analysis, pair financial totals with annual population to calculate spending per resident, but describe physical capacity per resident as **not quantified** unless a consistent licensed/staffed bed series and replacement/decommissioning data are added. Do not combine acute, long-term-care, addiction and surgical-room counts. The verified project examples demonstrate physical modernization and service-specific capacity expansion during UCP tenure, not a complete quantified net provincial hospital-capacity growth measure.
