# Independent report audit, round 2

Reviewed 9 October 2026. Scope: REPORT.md and README.md narrative against original official local PDF pages, alongside the independently verified financial series. No report text edited by reviewer.

## Overall finding

The draft's central answer is grounded: nominal capital spending recovered, but 2024–25 CPI-adjusted spending per resident remains 12.6% below 2018–19. The report distinguishes financial inputs from net capacity, grants from capital assets, construction completion from opening, party tenure from initiation, and the accessible lagged decade from the latest completed decade. It correctly declines to infer province-wide infrastructure adequacy. No causal or statistically significant party effect is asserted.

## Actionable corrections

**Medium: Grande Prairie phase dates.** The health table currently says “Phases 1 and 2 in 2021–22 completed-project list”. Original `2021-22 Budget.pdf` p23 lists **Phase 2**; original `2020-21 Budget.pdf` p23 lists **Phase 1** completed and Phase 2 still in progress. Combined $850M funding is correct. Change to “Phase 1 listed completed in 2020–21; Phase 2 in 2021–22; combined total capital-plan funding $850M” and cite both original pages. This matters because phase dates are an explicit deliverable timeline.

**Low: title endpoint scope.** The current title is broadly true for the main baseline but reads as if the pace result covers every post-2019 comparison. Anchor the title or immediately adjacent subtitle to **2018–19 versus 2024–25**. Suggested concise headline: “Alberta infrastructure spending recovered, but 2024–25 CPI-adjusted spending per resident was 12.6% below 2018–19”. The present “consumer prices” wording is preferable to an unqualified construction/infrastructure growth claim.

**Low: recovery-community citation.** Three existing communities / 200 beds and eight in progress / 500+ beds are directly supported by 2024–25 pp15 and20. The health source block's latest link lands p23, which does not contain the aggregate count. Add a p20 link next to this row or source block. The existing caveat preventing double counting Gunn's 100 beds is correct.

**Low: money sign and executive percentages.** Replace “a difference of $-1.04 billion” with “$1.04 billion below that benchmark” or “a signed difference of −$1.04 billion”. “Was -1.4% ... compared with” should read “was 1.4% lower than ...”; the current wording is understandable but treats a change as a level.

## Remaining limits that the report handles appropriately

1. Source actuals end31 March2025; latest completed fiscal decade ends2025–26. The missing year is explicitly missing rather than imputed. Online access blocker is reported as retrieval limitation, not claim that2025–26 data do not exist publicly.
2. Report-vintage rounded demographic inputs are not the latest StatsCan estimates; annual calendar-price versus fiscal spending alignment is disclosed.
3. Province warns of accounting-policy changes even in same-vintage historical table. Recent classification pairs avoid a naive decade ministry panel, and maintenance is separated from recent expansion envelopes.
4. Document construction/operational definitions change over time. Delivered project examples are not a complete provincial asset register.
5. No causal identification, construction-price deflation or net seats/beds/network/condition series. The central answer must continue to distinguish resource allocation intensity from adequate service provision.

Subject to the phase-date correction, the financial and sampled physical narrative is ready for final artifact/reproduction validation.
