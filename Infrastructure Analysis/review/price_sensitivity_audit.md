# Independent audit: hypothetical price sensitivity

**Result: passes mathematical, source-status and visual-semantic checks.** Reviewed `scripts/price_sensitivity.py`, all 351 rows of `data/hypothetical_price_scenarios.csv`, the rendered PNG, and report section 4. Root-owned scripts were not edited.

The curve fixes observed 2018-19 and 2024-25 capital spending and their same-vintage July population denominators. Its only varied quantity is assumed cumulative price growth. Independently recomputed every CSV point with `(endpoint nominal dollars/person ÷ baseline nominal dollars/person) / (1 + assumed price growth/100) - 1`; all reconcile within 1e-10 percentage points. The resulting reciprocal curve decreases monotonically and crosses zero at **5.0030134%** hypothetical cumulative price growth. The **5.0%** rendered label is correct rounding. Scenarios of 10% and 20% produce approximately **−4.5%** and **−12.5%**, matching report prose.

The x-axis explicitly says **Hypothetical cumulative price growth**. The y-axis says **Calculated price-adjusted capital spending per resident change**, rather than assets, service output or construction volume. The footer clearly distinguishes the illustrative curve from observed construction prices and construction-volume growth. The separately shaped gold square identifies the CPI chain (+20.1%) and its CPI-adjusted spending result (−12.6%); the footnote explains that consumer inflation is observed while suitable construction-price data remain unavailable. Technically the chained cumulative CPI factor is calculated from observed rounded annual rates, not an independently observed official index-level ratio; the report's methods and source notes explicitly disclose that approximation.

The image's annotations, zero crossing, labels and footer are readable and no material information is clipped. Report section 4 repeats that hypothetical scenarios are not observed construction inflation, explains population × CPI multiplicatively, and limits the benchmark to spending intensity rather than need or maintenance backlog. It does not equate the 5.0% threshold with actual construction inflation.

The executive statement that the 2019-20-baseline CPI-adjusted result is **1.4% lower and approximately flat given unresolved fiscal alignment and price-measure uncertainty** is appropriately cautious. “Approximately flat” is a descriptive approximation, not a statistical finding. Deterministic rounding alone retains a negative sign; broader fiscal-average population and price-selection uncertainty is unresolved. The report also retains the main pre-UCP 2018-19 baseline, avoiding use of the already-lower 2019-20 year to erase the initial decline.

July population is correctly identified as July 1 of the fiscal starting calendar year. It is a within-year snapshot, not an exact fiscal-average denominator or fiscal midpoint. The inspected analysis input has only **actual** capital spending statuses and calendar-year labels **2015 through 2024**. There are no 2025 observations in this scenario model. Report section 1 explicitly discloses the missing 2025-26 actual year and excludes 2026-29 projections from actual delivery evidence.

No blocking corrections are required. Remaining limitations are already disclosed: rounded CPI chaining, quarterly/monthly fiscal alignment unavailable, construction prices unavailable, and a spending basket containing differing infrastructure/grant/equipment components. These restrict claims about physical growth, not the validity of this clearly labelled arithmetic sensitivity.
