# README style guide

Audience: a reader with Grade 12 English who wants to understand Alberta's spending, public-service results and living conditions without specialist training.

## Voice and paragraph structure

- Use clear Canadian English, active voice and complete sentences. Correct spelling, punctuation and grammar without making the tone academic or promotional.
- Lead each topic with its main finding. Explain the key comparison, its meaning and its limits in that order.
- Keep paragraphs focused, usually two to four sentences. Aim for sentences of about 15–25 words, while retaining longer sentences when needed for precise definitions. Do not chase a readability score by breaking sound explanations into fragments.
- Use familiar wording. Explain essential terms where readers meet them: inflation adjustment, median, 90th percentile, reporting baseline and measure denominator. Avoid unexplained abbreviations and bureaucratic wording.
- End the visible topic with a concise synthesis when it adds meaning. Describe what the evidence supports; do not invent an expert consensus.
- Avoid repeating the same number or caveat unnecessarily. Keep detail in existing expandable evidence sections, with the key finding and interpretation visible.

## Evidence and interpretation

- Preserve every reported number, sign, unit, baseline, target, fiscal/calendar/school year and qualifying definition. Do not add forecasts, extrapolate beyond observed periods or claim a trend from one observation.
- Distinguish actual spending, budget amounts, forecasts, estimates, targets and hypothetical scenarios.
- Separate financial inputs, project outputs, access measures and population outcomes. A spending increase is not proof of improved services; an outcome change does not establish a government's effect.
- Compare compatible populations and reporting periods. Keep the two poverty bases separate. Preserve missing observations, preliminary life tables and source uncertainty.
- Keep material limitations beside their findings, using plain explanations. Preserve the completed-surgery denominator, site coverage, education scope, combined housing measure and reporting lags.
- Original archive figures and policy scenarios have different definitions and review coverage. Identify them as historical material; do not present unverified assumptions as established findings.

## Numbers, punctuation and presentation

- Use an en dash in ranges and fiscal years: 2018–19, 2021–22 to 2024–25. Use a true minus sign for negative changes and preserve meaningful precision.
- In prose, prefer “rose from... to...” over arrows. Use percentage points for differences between rates; use percent for relative change. Do not compute additional changes unless checked from the source values.
- State Canadian dollars once in the scope. Preserve existing dollar abbreviations and financial table cells. Spell out a unit when needed to understand a comparison.
- Bold only major findings, dates or short labels. Avoid bolding whole paragraphs, unnecessary headings or repeated “takeaway” boxes.
- Keep the existing section headings, links, chart paths, table cells, `<details>` structure and seven visible-chart layout. Alternative text should describe the actual graph in at least eight words.
- Keep blank lines around paragraphs, lists, tables, images and expandable blocks. All 42 charts must remain accessible exactly once.
